HPV, the HPV Vaccine & Cervical Screening: A Practical Guide

Preventive gynecologic care is about more than periods. HPV vaccination and cervical screening can reduce cervical cancer risk.

Updated: September 24, 2026 · Educational information only

What HPV is

Human papillomavirus (HPV) is a common infection. Some HPV types can cause genital warts, while certain high-risk types can lead to cervical and other cancers.

Why vaccination matters

CDC guidance says HPV vaccination is safe and effective and can protect against diseases, including cancers, caused by HPV when given in recommended age groups.

Vaccination does not replace screening

ACOG notes that people who have been vaccinated against HPV still need to follow cervical cancer screening recommendations for their age group. The vaccine does not cover every cancer-causing HPV type.

Screening is about finding changes early

Cervical screening may involve a Pap test, HPV testing or both, depending on age and the guideline used in your country. Screening recommendations vary by country, age, history and risk factors.

Keep your local schedule in mind

Because screening systems differ, use national guidance or your clinician’s recommendation rather than copying a schedule from another country’s website.
