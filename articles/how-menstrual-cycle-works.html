CYCLE BASICS

How the Menstrual Cycle Works

A practical guide to cycle counting, the main phases, and why real-world timing can change.

Cycle basicsTrackingEstimates

The menstrual cycle is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next period. A commonly cited adult reference range is around 21–35 days, while bleeding often lasts up to about 7 days. Individual patterns can still vary.

1. Menstruation

Menstruation is the bleeding phase of the cycle. The uterine lining is shed when pregnancy has not occurred. The first day of bleeding is used as cycle day 1.

2. The follicular phase

After menstruation begins, hormonal changes support development of an ovarian follicle. The length of this part of the cycle can vary, which is one reason the total cycle length may change.

3. Ovulation

Ovulation is the release of an egg from an ovary. Its timing is not identical for everyone and can shift from cycle to cycle. Calendar methods can estimate a likely time, but they cannot confirm that ovulation actually happened.

4. The luteal phase

After ovulation, the body enters the luteal phase. If pregnancy does not occur, hormone levels change and the next period begins.

Why tracking helps

Recording the first day of each period over several months can make your personal pattern easier to see and can give a healthcare professional useful information if your cycle changes.
