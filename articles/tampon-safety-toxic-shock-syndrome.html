Tampon Safety & Toxic Shock Syndrome: What Women Should Know

Toxic shock syndrome is rare, but tampon safety is worth understanding.

Updated: September 24, 2026 · Educational information only

What is toxic shock syndrome?

Toxic shock syndrome (TSS) is a rare but serious illness caused by bacterial toxins. Menstrual TSS has been associated with tampon use, particularly when tampons are used in ways that increase risk.

Safer tampon habits

Follow the product instructions, choose an absorbency appropriate for your flow and change tampons regularly. The Office on Women’s Health advises that tampons should not be worn for more than 8 hours.

Do not use more absorbency than you need

Higher absorbency is not automatically safer or better. Choose based on your menstrual flow and the instructions for the product.

Know the warning signs

Possible TSS symptoms include sudden fever, vomiting, diarrhea, muscle aches, dizziness or fainting and a rash resembling sunburn. TSS can become serious quickly.

If you feel suddenly very unwell

If you develop sudden severe symptoms while using a tampon or other inserted menstrual product, remove the product and seek urgent medical care. Tell the healthcare professional that you were using a menstrual product.

Other inserted period products

Menstrual cups and some other vaginally inserted products also require careful cleaning and use according to instructions. Always follow the product maker’s safety guidance.
