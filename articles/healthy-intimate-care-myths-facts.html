10 Common Intimate Health Myths: What Is Actually True?

Good intimate care is usually gentle, evidence-based and much less complicated than social media suggests.

Updated: September 24, 2026 · Educational information only

Myth 1: A healthy vagina should have no odor

Fact: Mild natural odor can be normal. A sudden strong or unpleasant odor can signal a problem that deserves assessment.

Myth 2: Douching keeps the vagina cleaner

Fact: The vagina cleans itself naturally. Douching can disturb its normal balance and cause irritation.

Myth 3: Pubic hair is unhygienic

Fact: Pubic hair is normal and there is no medical need to remove it.

Myth 4: Hymen appearance proves virginity

Fact: The WHO says no known examination can prove a history of vaginal intercourse, and hymen appearance is not a reliable indicator.

Myth 5: White discharge always means infection

Fact: Clear-to-white discharge without a noticeable odor can be normal, and discharge naturally changes during the menstrual cycle.

Myth 6: Every itch is a yeast infection

Fact: Yeast, BV, STIs, irritation and skin conditions can overlap in symptoms. Diagnosis matters.

Myth 7: A tampon can get lost inside the body

Fact: A tampon cannot travel beyond the vagina, but a forgotten tampon should be removed and medical help sought if it cannot be removed.

Myth 8: More absorbent menstrual products are always better

Fact: Use the absorbency appropriate for your flow and follow product instructions.

Myth 9: HPV vaccination means cervical screening is unnecessary

Fact: Vaccination does not cover every cancer-causing HPV type, so screening still matters according to local guidelines.

Myth 10: Painful periods must simply be tolerated

Fact: Severe, worsening or disabling pain deserves medical evaluation.
