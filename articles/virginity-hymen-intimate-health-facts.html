Virginity, the Hymen & Intimate Health: Facts Every Woman Should Know

A respectful, evidence-based guide to virginity myths, hymen facts and healthy intimate care.

Updated: September 24, 2026 · Educational information only

Virginity is not a medical condition

“Virginity” is a social, cultural or personal concept rather than a medical diagnosis. There is no physical examination that can reliably establish whether someone has had vaginal intercourse.

What the hymen actually is

The hymen is a thin, elastic rim of tissue around part of the vaginal opening. Its shape, thickness and amount of tissue vary naturally. Some people have very little visible hymenal tissue, while others have more.

Can the hymen prove intercourse?

No. The appearance of a hymen cannot reliably show whether vaginal intercourse has occurred. The World Health Organization states that so-called virginity testing has no scientific merit or clinical indication, and that no known examination can prove a history of vaginal intercourse.

Does first sex always cause bleeding?

No. Bleeding is not a reliable sign of first vaginal intercourse. A person may or may not bleed, and the presence or absence of blood cannot establish sexual history.

How to protect intimate health

Healthy care is about protecting the vulvar skin and the vagina’s natural environment—not trying to change or “preserve” a hymen. Gentle washing of the vulva, avoiding douching and strongly fragranced products, and getting medical advice for persistent itching, burning, sores, unusual discharge or pain are more meaningful health steps.

Important reminder

No person owes anyone proof of virginity. Concerns about sexual history, pressure, coercion or safety are different from ordinary health questions and may require trusted support or professional care.
