STI Prevention & Testing: Practical Sexual Health Basics

Sexual health is part of overall health, even when symptoms are absent.

Updated: September 24, 2026 · Educational information only

Many STIs cause no symptoms

CDC guidance emphasizes that a person can have an STI without knowing it. Feeling well is not the same as having a confirmed negative test.

Testing is the only way to know your status

The right tests depend on sexual activity, body sites exposed, symptoms, pregnancy status and local clinical guidance. Ask a qualified healthcare professional which tests are appropriate.

Condoms reduce risk

Using condoms correctly every time can lower the risk of many STIs. They do not eliminate all risk because some infections, including HPV and herpes, can spread through skin-to-skin contact in areas a condom does not cover.

Vaccination can prevent some infections

CDC recommends vaccines for some people to prevent hepatitis B and HPV. Eligibility and timing depend on age and individual circumstances.

If you may have been exposed

Do not wait for symptoms before asking about testing or treatment. Some infections are treatable, and early evaluation can reduce complications and transmission to partners.

Privacy and consent matter

Healthy sexual decision-making includes clear consent, the ability to say no, and access to confidential healthcare. Seek help from a trusted professional if you are experiencing pressure, coercion or violence.

CycleCare note: This guide is for general education. It does not diagnose a condition or replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
