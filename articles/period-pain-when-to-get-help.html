Period Pain: What Can Be Normal and When to Get Help

A clear guide to common menstrual cramps, warning signs, and useful tracking information.

Period painSymptomsWhen to seek care

Why periods can hurt

Period pain is common and often happens because the womb tightens during menstruation. NHS guidance describes this as a common part of the menstrual cycle.

When pain may need more attention

Medical assessment is especially important when pain is severe, worse than usual, does not improve with usual measures, or interferes with normal activities. NHS guidance also recommends assessment when periods become more painful, heavier or irregular, or when there is pain during sex or while using the toilet.

Possible causes of more troublesome pain

Persistent or unusually severe pain can sometimes be associated with conditions such as endometriosis, adenomyosis, fibroids or pelvic inflammatory disease. These conditions require professional assessment rather than a calendar calculator.

Why symptom tracking can help

Record the start of pain, how long it lasts, its severity, bleeding changes, and whether it affects work or school. A period and symptom diary can give a healthcare professional a clearer picture of your pattern.

Do not self-diagnose from a cycle app

A period tracker can organize dates and symptoms, but it cannot identify the cause of pain. A new or worsening pattern deserves proper medical evaluation.

Get help urgently: Seek urgent medical care for severe or rapidly worsening pelvic pain, especially when accompanied by other concerning symptoms or possible pregnancy.
