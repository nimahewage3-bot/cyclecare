PERIOD TRACKING

How to Calculate Your Next Period

Understand the simple calendar calculation used by CycleCare and its limitations.

Period trackingCalendar methodPlanning

The basic calculation

To make a simple calendar estimate, start with the first day of your latest period and add your average cycle length. For example, a 28-day average gives an estimate roughly 28 days after the previous period started.

What “cycle length” means

Cycle length is measured from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. It is not the same as the number of days you bleed.

Why your estimate may be early or late

The method assumes the upcoming cycle will resemble the average used in the calculation. Actual timing can vary, so one predicted date should never be treated as a guaranteed result.

How to get a better personal baseline

Track the start date of each period for several cycles. A record of your own pattern is more useful than assuming every cycle is exactly 28 days. Tracking can also help you explain changes to a healthcare professional.

When a change deserves attention

ACOG notes that cycles outside the usual range, bleeding lasting more than 7 days, very heavy bleeding, or long gaps without a period can be reasons to seek medical evaluation.

CycleCare note: This calculator is for planning and educational purposes. It does not diagnose menstrual disorders and does not guarantee a future period date.
