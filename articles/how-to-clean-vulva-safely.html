How to Clean Your Vulva Safely

Good intimate hygiene is usually simpler than advertising makes it seem.

Updated: September 24, 2026 · Educational information only

First, know the difference

The vulva is the external genital area. The vagina is the internal canal. These areas are cared for differently.

Wash the outside gently

For most people, gentle rinsing of the vulva with warm water is enough. ACOG notes that plain water is adequate, especially when the skin is irritated. If soap is used, choose a mild, fragrance-free product and keep it on the external skin rather than inside the vagina.

Do not douche

The vagina cleans itself naturally. Douching or flushing water, soaps or solutions inside the vagina can disturb the normal balance of microorganisms and may increase irritation or vaginitis risk.

Skip scented intimate products

Perfumed sprays, deodorants, scented wipes and heavily fragranced soaps can irritate sensitive genital skin. A strong odor that appears suddenly is better assessed medically than covered with fragrance.

Keep the area dry and comfortable

After bathing or exercise, gently pat the vulva dry. Changing out of wet clothing and choosing breathable underwear can help reduce moisture and friction.

Know when self-care is not enough

Persistent itching, burning, swelling, sores, a new lump, pain, bleeding or a noticeable change in discharge or odor should be assessed rather than treated repeatedly with home products.
