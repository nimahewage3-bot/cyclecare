What Is Ovulation?

Understand ovulation, its relationship to the menstrual cycle, and the limits of calendar-based predictions.

OvulationFertilityCycle timing

What ovulation means

Ovulation is the release of an egg from an ovary. The timing can vary between people and from one cycle to another.

How calendar estimates work

A calendar calculator can estimate ovulation by working backward from a predicted next period. A common simple assumption is that ovulation occurs about 14 days before the next period. ACOG explains why the day can shift depending on cycle length. citeturn872848search1

Signs people may notice

Some people notice changes in cervical mucus around ovulation. ACOG notes that cervical mucus often changes and can peak around the time of ovulation. These signs can vary, so they should not be treated as a guaranteed indicator on their own. citeturn872848search1

Ovulation pain

Some people experience brief one-sided lower abdominal discomfort around ovulation. NHS guidance says it can last from a few minutes to 1–2 days, but severe, persistent or recurring pain should be assessed. citeturn872848search2

Why the fertile window is an estimate

A predicted fertile window is based on assumptions about when ovulation may happen. Because actual ovulation can vary, CycleCare should not be used as a stand-alone method of contraception or as proof that a particular day is fertile or infertile.

CycleCare note: Our ovulation result is a calendar estimate, not a confirmation of ovulation.
