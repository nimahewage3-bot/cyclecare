Pelvic Floor Health: Kegels, Bladder Control & When to Get Help

Pelvic floor muscles support several pelvic organs and can affect bladder control, childbirth recovery and sexual comfort.

Updated: September 24, 2026 · Educational information only

What the pelvic floor does

Pelvic floor muscles support the bladder, uterus, vagina and rectum and help control urine and stool. They are active in everyday movement as well as during pregnancy and after childbirth.

What are Kegel exercises?

Kegels are exercises that tighten and relax the pelvic floor muscles. They are commonly used for pelvic floor muscle training and may improve some forms of urinary leakage.

Basic technique

One common approach is to gently squeeze the pelvic floor for about 3 seconds, relax for 3 seconds and repeat. Gradually work toward up to 3 sets of 10 repetitions a day if a healthcare professional has advised pelvic floor training.

Do not practice while urinating

Regularly stopping urine flow in the middle of urination is not recommended as an exercise because it can interfere with normal bladder function.

More is not always better

Some people have pelvic floor muscles that are too tight rather than too weak. In those cases, repeated strengthening exercises can worsen symptoms. Pain, difficulty relaxing, painful intercourse or some urinary symptoms may need a pelvic-floor assessment instead.

When to seek guidance

See a clinician or pelvic-floor physical therapist if you are unsure which muscles to use, have pelvic pain, painful sex, persistent urinary problems, or symptoms that worsen with exercises.
