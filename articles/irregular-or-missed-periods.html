Irregular or Missed Periods: What to Know

Understand why cycle timing can change and when it is worth discussing a pattern with a healthcare professional.

Irregular periodsMissed periodsCycle tracking

What counts as irregular?

There is not one single pattern that fits everyone. ACOG describes typical adult cycles as about 21–35 days and identifies cycles outside that range, bleeding longer than 7 days, or very heavy bleeding as situations that may need evaluation.

Why a period can be late or missed

Cycle changes can have many possible causes, including stress, changes in weight, hormonal conditions, some medicines, pregnancy, breastfeeding, perimenopause and other health issues. The right explanation depends on the person and situation.

Why tracking matters

Write down the start date of each period and note major changes in bleeding, pain or other symptoms. Tracking can help you recognize a new pattern and can provide useful information during a medical visit.

When to talk to a clinician

Consider getting medical advice when periods that were previously regular become irregular, when cycles repeatedly fall outside your usual pattern, when there is unusually heavy or prolonged bleeding, or when you go for several months without a period.

What CycleCare can and cannot do

CycleCare can organize cycle dates and provide calendar estimates. It cannot determine why a period is late, diagnose a condition, or replace a medical assessment.

Important: A missed or late period can have many causes. A calculator result should not be used to decide that a medical problem is or is not present.
