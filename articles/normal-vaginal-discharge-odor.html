Normal Vaginal Discharge & Odor: What to Know

Discharge is a normal body function. The key is noticing changes from your usual pattern.

Updated: September 24, 2026 · Educational information only

Normal discharge is common

Vaginal discharge begins around puberty and helps keep the vagina moist and healthy. Normal discharge can be clear or white and may be thick, sticky, slippery or wet.

It can change during the cycle

The amount and texture of discharge can vary with hormonal changes. Around ovulation, discharge may become clearer, wetter or more noticeable.

What about vaginal odor?

A mild natural odor can be normal. A new, strong or unpleasant odor—especially when it comes with another change—can be a reason to get checked.

Changes worth discussing

Seek medical advice if discharge changes in color, smell, amount or consistency, or if you also have itching, soreness, pain when urinating, pelvic pain, bleeding between periods or bleeding after sex.

Do not try to “reset” the vagina

Scented products, deodorants and douches do not make an unhealthy vagina healthier. They can irritate the vulva or disturb the normal vaginal environment.

Track your normal

Knowing what is usual for you can make it easier to notice a meaningful change. A simple note of discharge, cycle day and symptoms can also make a medical appointment more useful.
