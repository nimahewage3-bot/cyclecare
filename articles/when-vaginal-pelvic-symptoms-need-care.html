When Vaginal or Pelvic Symptoms Need Medical Attention

Knowing when a symptom has moved beyond normal variation can help you seek care at the right time.

Updated: September 24, 2026 · Educational information only

Persistent itching or burning

Ongoing itching, burning, redness, swelling or irritation can come from infection, dermatitis or other conditions. Persistent symptoms deserve an assessment instead of repeated use of random products.

New lumps, sores or skin changes

ACOG recommends medical review for new bumps, painful swelling, persistent itching or burning, and meaningful changes in vulvar skin.

Unusual discharge or odor

A noticeable change in color, smell, amount or consistency—especially with pelvic pain or bleeding—can need testing to identify the cause.

Bleeding outside your usual period

Bleeding between periods or after sex can have several causes. NHS guidance recommends evaluation when these changes occur.

Pelvic pain

Severe, persistent or worsening pelvic pain should be assessed. Urgent care is especially important when pain is sudden or severe, or when it occurs with fainting, fever, heavy bleeding or possible pregnancy.

Heavy menstrual bleeding

Very heavy bleeding can include needing to change a pad or tampon every 1–2 hours, bleeding longer than 7 days, passing large clots or feeling tired or short of breath. These patterns can justify medical evaluation.

Prepare for the appointment

Write down when symptoms started, the cycle day, bleeding pattern, pain severity, medications and any recent changes. Clear notes can help a clinician work through possible causes more efficiently.

CycleCare note: This guide is for general education. It does not diagnose a condition or replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
